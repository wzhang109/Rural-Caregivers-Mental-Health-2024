# Socioeconomic status and caregiver mental health in rural China

**Wenwen Zhang · University of San Francisco · International and Development Economics**

This repository archives my master's thesis on household socioeconomic status
and self-reported distress among primary caregivers in rural Ya’an, China.
The thesis asks how household income and caregiver education are associated
with depression, anxiety and stress scores in the setting of an early-childhood
development project.

[Read the original thesis PDF](paper/USF_Master_Thesis_Wenwen_Zhang_2024.pdf)

## Research scope

The thesis describes 2023 baseline cross-sectional data and reports OLS analyses
of income, education and DASS-21 outcomes, with caregiver-role comparisons and
an age-based sample restriction. The reported main associations are negative:
higher income and education are associated with lower reported distress scores.
These analyses do not identify causal effects of income, education or the
parenting-center intervention.

The data came from the setting of a broader intervention project. Randomization
of an intervention does not make the observational income and education
comparisons randomized. The thesis is not an evaluation of that intervention's
treatment effect or an estimate of effects on children's mental health.


## Files and reproducibility

- `paper/` contains the archival thesis.
- `docs/` records the scope of the review and what still needs verification.

No respondent-level survey data or private administrative records are included.
The thesis itself is the original academic work. AI assistance was used for the
2026 document review and repository description; this does not imply that a
new analysis has been independently validated or change the authorship of the
original thesis.
